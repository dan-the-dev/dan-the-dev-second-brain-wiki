## Introduction to the Project Aristotele Study
- The speaker, Carlo Lagorio, a Scrum Master and Agile Coach, introduces the topic of the five fundamental characteristics of an efficient team, which will be explored through the analysis of the Project Aristotele study.
- The study, conducted by [[google|Google]] in 2016, aimed to identify the common characteristics of its most performing teams, and it was named after the Greek philosopher [[aristotle|Aristotle]], who said "the whole is greater than the sum of its parts", representing the essence of a team and the difference between an efficient and non-efficient team.
- The Project Aristotele study, which lasted for a year and a half and involved around 160 teams, found some surprising results, including the fact that certain variables, such as the team's location, decision-making process, extroversion of team members, individual intelligence, performance, workload, years of experience, team size, and years spent in the same company, were not significant in determining a team's efficiency.
- The speaker notes that the most surprising non-significant variable is individual intelligence, but it makes sense because a highly intelligent person does not necessarily mean they are a good team player, and if they do not work well in a team, the team's performance will suffer.

- The first characteristic of an effective team is the ability of each individual to speak and make mistakes without fear of consequences, which is closely related to the concept of psychological safety.
- The second characteristic of an effective team is reliability, which means that team members can trust each other to fulfill their commitments and complete tasks on time and to a high standard, and this reliability is a two-way street, where each member must be reliable for their colleagues.
- The third characteristic of an effective team is a clear structure and organization, which includes having a well-defined objective and a clear decision-making process, with specific individuals having the power to make decisions, and this structure should enable the team to achieve its goals efficiently.
